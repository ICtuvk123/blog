Background

Patient prognosis in oncology refers to the predicted progression and outcome of cancer, often determined by clinical, pathological and molecular factors Recently, integrating multiple data modalities have been shown to improve survival prediction in cancer research, with most studies focusing on combining genomic data such as transcriptomic data with histology whole slide images (WSIs) However, pathology reports remain an under-utilized resource despite providing essential information to clinicians for estimating the prognosis of cancer patients

Related work

multimodal

Early studies primarily relied on late fusion using techniques such as concatenation [39], Kronecker product [3, 64] or factorized bilinear pooling [31, 45]. problem: since late fusion merges unimodal representations only at the final stage [12], it fails to capture cross-modal interactions, leading to suboptimal integration.

Many models utilize transformers [66], incorporating co-attention [5, 34, 74] and cross-attention mechanisms [9, 20, 37, 51] to enhance feature integration. problem: genomics and pathology reports is modality imbalance, arising from differences in structure and scale of the data modalities.
