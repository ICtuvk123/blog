Background

problems

  1. First, a sample from DM is generated by integrating a stochastic differential equation (SDE), so the generated sample becomes a stochastic, non-invertible mapping of the latent variables
  2. Further, the extra uncertainty injected by the stochastic drift can make it more difficult to leverage the commonly used weak supervision.

our solution

  1. proposing notions of approximate disentanglement applicable to stochastic, non-invertible settings
  2. marrying information-regularised score matching with recent finite-sample analyses of score-based models

preliminary

Diffusion process

Diffusion models (DMs) [31–33] approximate the pdf $p_X =: p_0$ of an r.v. $X \sim p_0$ via a two-stage process: _noising_ and _denoising_. In the noising stage, data is progressively corrupted using an SDE:

$$dX_t = \mu(X_t, t)dt + \xi(t)dB_t, \quad X_0 \sim p_0, \quad (1)$$

where $B_t$ is a Brownian motion. We adopt the choices$\mu(X_t, t) := -X_t$ and $\xi(t) \equiv \sqrt{2}$, leading to the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process:

$$dX_t = -X_t dt + \sqrt{2}dB_t, \quad X_0 \sim p_0, \quad (2)$$

which converges to a standard Gaussian [42, 41]. Let$p_t$ denote the marginal of$X_t$ and$p_{t|s}$the conditional pdf of$X_t$ given$X_s$. In the denoising stage, the goal is to recover$X$from noisy versions$X_t, t \geq t_0$by simulating the time-reversed process$X_t^{\leftarrow} := X_{T-t}$

$$dX_t^{\leftarrow} = [X_t^{\leftarrow} + 2\nabla_x \log p_{T-t}(X_t^{\leftarrow})]dt + \sqrt{2}dB_t^{\leftarrow}, \quad X_0^{\leftarrow} \sim p_T, \quad (3)$$

which converges back to $p_0$[43]. Since the score function$\nabla_x \log p_t(X_t)$ is unknown, DM learns a score estimator$s_\theta : \mathbb{R}^{d_x} \times [0, T] \mapsto \mathbb{R}^{d_x}$ by minimizing the score matching objective:

$$L(\theta) := \mathbb{E}_{t, p_t} \|s_\theta(X_t, t) - \nabla_x \log p_t(X_t)\|^2,$$

This objective is equivalent to a _conditional_ score matching objective involving$p_{t|0}$ $$L_c(\theta) := \mathbb{E}_{t, p_0 p_{t|0}} \|s_\theta(X_t, t) - \nabla_x \log p_{t|0}(X_t | X)\|^2 = \mathbb{E}_{t, p_0 p_{t|0}} \left\|s_\theta(X_t, t) + \frac{N_t}{\sigma(t)}\right\|^2, \quad (4)$$

where$N_t$ is standard Gaussian and$\sigma(t) := \sqrt{1 - \exp(-2t)}$. During inference, new samples are generated by simulating an estimated SDE, with $\hat{X}_0^{\leftarrow} \sim p_T$ and discretized time steps$t \in [k\eta, (k+1)\eta]$: $$d\hat{X}_t^{\leftarrow} = [\hat{X}_t^{\leftarrow} + 2s_\theta(\hat{X}_{k\eta}^{\leftarrow}, T - k\eta)]dt + \sqrt{2}dB_t^{\leftarrow}. \quad (5)$$

(ε,ν)-disentanglement

To quantify how well the learned content and style representations are separated and informative, we define an information-theoretic notion of approximate disentanglement. Let $(\hat{Z},\hat{G})$ be content and style encodings inferred from an observed sample X. They are (ε,ν)-disentangled if, for some ε,ν ≥ 0, (i)$I(\hat{Z}; \hat{G}) \leq \epsilon$; (ii)$I(\hat{Z}, \hat{G}; X) \geq I(Z, G; X) - \nu$, where $I(A;B)$ denotes the mutual information (MI) between r.v.’s A and B

$\epsilon$-editability

In many applications, it is desirable to modify style while preserving content. This motivates the following notion of editability based on conditional sample generation. Definition 3.2 ($\epsilon$-editability): Let $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G})$ be encodings inferred from $X$, and let $\hat{G}' \sim p_{\hat{G}}$ be an independent style encoding. $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G})$ are $\epsilon$-editable if there exists a generative model $q(\cdot | \hat{Z}, \hat{G}')$ such that the generated sample $\hat{X} \sim q(\cdot | \hat{Z}, \hat{G}')$ satisfies:

$\mathbb{E}_{Z \sim p_Z} d_{TV} (p_{\tilde{X}|Z}, p_{\hat{X}|Z}) \leq \epsilon$

where $\tilde{X} := \sqrt{1-\delta^2}X + \delta N$ is a smoothed version of $X$, and $d_{TV}$ is the Total Variation (TV) distance.

The notions of (ε,ν)-disentanglement and ε′-editability are complimentary but not equivalent.

The following example illustrates this by constructing encodings that are perfectly disentangled (i.e., (0, 0)-disentangled) yet fail the editability criterion due to a hidden ambiguity introduced during recombination.

Content disentanglement Assume Eq. 6, and suppose a known style function $g : \mathbb{R}^{d_X} \mapsto \mathbb{R}^{d_G}$ is given such that $g(f(z, G)) = G$ for all $z$. The goal of content disentanglement is to learn encodings $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G})$ from $X$ that are $(\epsilon, \nu)$-disentangled and $\epsilon'$-editable.

Multi-view disentanglement Assume there are multiple views $X^1, \dots, X^{n_V}$, where each $X^i$ is generated as: $$X^i = \sqrt{1-\delta_i^2} f_i(Z, G^i) + \delta_i N^i, \quad 1 \leq i \leq n_V$$ The task of multi-view disentanglement is to learn encodings $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G}^i)$ for all $i$ such that $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G}^i)$ are $(\epsilon, \nu)$-disentangled and $\epsilon'$-editable.

Assumption 3.5. The sample X is sub-gaussian with second moment $σ_{X}^2dX$ . Assumption 3.6. The score function of the sample pdf $p_X$ is $λ_s-Lipschitz$.

Assumption 3.5 ensures that sample values do not exhibit heavy tails, which could destabilize the diffusion process. Assumption 3.6 ensures that the score function does not change too abruptly, preventing discontinuities that could hinder accurate recovery of content and style during denoising

Theorem 4.1 (Diffusion-based Disentanglement)

Regularized Score Matching Objective:

The model is trained by minimizing the following objective function:

$$L_c^{\gamma,\rho}(\theta, \phi) := \mathbb{E}_{t, p_0, p_{t|0}} \left\| s_\theta(X_t, \hat{Z}, \hat{G}, t) + \frac{N_t}{\sigma(t)} \right\|^2 + \gamma(I(z_\phi(X_{t_0}); X) - \rho)_+ \tag{7}$$

Theorem Statement:

Suppose Assumption 3.5-3.6 hold, and let $(\theta^, \phi^)$ be a minimizer of $L_c^{\gamma,\rho}$ as defined in Eq. (7).

Set the following hyperparameters and conditions:

  • $\rho = I(Z;X) + C_1 \delta$
  • $\gamma \ge C_2/T$ for some constants $C_1, C_2 > 0$
  • $t_0 = -\log(1-\delta^2)^{1/2}$ and $\hat{Z} := z_{\phi^*}(X)$

Then for any noise level $\delta < \min \{ \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_x}} \}$, the resulting encodings $(\hat{Z}, \hat{G}) = (z_{\phi^*}(X_{t_0}), g(X_{t_0}))$ are $(\epsilon, \nu)$-disentangled with:

$$\epsilon = C_3 \lambda_s \sigma_X^2 d_x \delta, \quad \nu = C_4 \sigma_X^2 \delta^2$$

where $C_3, C_4 > 0$ are constants.
