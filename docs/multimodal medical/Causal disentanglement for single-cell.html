Background

Motivation

  1. Conducting disentanglement learning on single-cell omics data offers a promising alternative to traditional black-box representation learning by separating the semantic concepts embedded in a biological process.

Problem

  1. Although single-cell data can measure gene expression levels, important concept information (cell type, batch effects) often remains hidden within the data
  2. the complexity of single-cell omics data, which is characterised by high dimensionality and the presence of entangled concepts
  3. However, disentanglement is very challenging for single-cell data, as sequencing technology often introduces noise, such as dropout effects, and the underlying biological concepts cannot be directly observed, unlike in image data.
  4. latent concepts such as parasite infection and cell status - in single-cell data are often causally connected, making it hard to clearly separate them with existing disentanglement methods.

Related work

Statistical methods:

factor analysis or nonnegative matrix factorisation are used to identify various biological programmes on the basis of statistical patterns However, these methods operate purely at a correlational level and do not account for causal relationships between biological concepts. In addition, they cannot support controlled manipulation of these concepts or perform counterfactual generation

Learning-based methods:

These approaches are generative and often utilise variational autoencoder (VAE)-based methods to learn hidden concepts by reconstructing single-cell data For example, CPA decouples perturbation responses11, scDisInFact removes batch effects9, and Biolord disentangles the concepts contained in single-cell data3. However, these methods operate under the assumption of independence between concepts and cannot guarantee the causality of concepts In addition, most methods rely on latent optimisation12, which can result in a loss of fine-grained concept representations at the single-cell resolution level

Benefit of disentanglement in the diffusion model

Therefore, the diffusion model has a natural information bottleneck and is a good inductive bias for disentanglement representation purposes. As different concepts to be disentangled may contain different amounts of information, the diffusion model can dynamically allocate this information to the latent concepts in the reverse diffusion step, where the information decreases as the number of time steps increases. This optimisation objective is then similar to that of AnnealVAE.
